Field report · Vatnajökull ice cap

    The ice we measured twice.

    Eleven winters of stake readings, drone surveys and one very patient camp cook. A long read on how a glacier tongue came to lose forty metres in a decade.

    i

    Forty-two stakes, one dead drill.

    Every September the team walks the same line of steel stakes drilled into the Breiðamerkurjökull tongue. Each is read to the nearest centimetre against a painted mark, then photographed from two angles.

    In 2019 the main steam drill failed in a crevasse field and the crew improvised with a hand auger and nine litres of boiling meltwater. The readings from that season are the least tidy and the most quoted.

    A stake does not lie. It only leans.

    Leaning is the problem: after a warm spell, stakes tilt in softening ice and the apparent loss shrinks. We now correct every reading with an inclinometer and note the correction in the margin.

    ii

    Where the water goes.

    Meltwater does not simply run downhill. It drains into moulins, travels beneath the glacier for weeks and reappears at the terminus carrying silt, which is why the lagoon at Jökulsárlón is the colour of milky tea.

    1.9 maverage surface lowering, 2023
    41 mterminus retreat since 2014

    The lagoon itself is a measurement: deeper than 280 metres at its centre and still deepening as the ice recedes over what used to be dry rock.

    iii

    Life at the Esjufjöll hut.

    The hut sleeps six and has a gas stove that works one day in three. Water is melted from snow, and the daily bread is pan-fried flatkaka that the cook, Sólrún, makes without measuring.

    Days start at 05:30 in September. Weather decides the schedule: a low cloud means no drone flights and a day of manual stake reading with ice axes and crampons.

    If the radio is quiet, the weather is fine. If it is chatty, stay in.

    iv

    What the model got wrong.

    The first mass-balance model underestimated summer melt by almost 18 percent because it assumed a clean white surface. Dust from Icelandic ash plains darkens the ice, and darker ice absorbs far more sunlight.

    18%underestimate in v1

    The revised model takes a weekly albedo reading from a handheld sensor carried across the same transect, and it now sits within five percent of the stake readings.

    v

    The line we will keep walking.

    Funding runs to 2030. Beyond that, the stakes will be handed to the regional park service and the dataset to the national archive in Reykjavik, with the notes in both English and Icelandic.

    If you want to join a field season, applications open each January for two volunteer places. Carrying skills matter more than science degrees.
