Saltworks of the Guérande01 / 05
i
Chapter one · March

Flat water, patient clay.

The paludiers rake the clay basins clean before the first spring tide. Nothing is added; the marsh is only asked to hold still.

ii
Chapter two · May

Seven ponds, one slope.

Seawater crosses a gradient of barely two centimetres per kilometre, warming and thickening as it travels toward the crystallising beds.

iii
Chapter three · July

The long rake at noon.

Fleur de sel forms in the hour the wind drops. It is skimmed with a wooden lousse, never touched by metal, and carried off in shallow trays.

iv
Chapter four · September

Grey salt, late light.

Coarse grey crystals settle on the clay floor and are drawn toward the mound in a single sweeping stroke, ten tonnes before dusk.

v
Chapter five · Winter

Rest, then repair.

Dikes are re-packed by hand, ponds are flooded to protect the clay, and the cooperative opens its ledger for the coming season.
