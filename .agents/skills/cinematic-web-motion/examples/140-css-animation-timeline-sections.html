Wyvern Hill Orchard Co-op · Harvest Journal

Two hundred apples nobody sells.

Forty growers in Herefordshire keep the old cider varieties alive: Foxwhelp, Kingston Black, Slack-ma-Girdle. None of them look good in a supermarket and all of them taste of something.

01 · Trees

Standards, not dwarfs.

Our trees are full-size standards, grafted onto seedling rootstock and left to grow for twelve years before the first useful crop. Sheep graze underneath; the grass is mown only once, in August.

That slowness is the point. A standard tree lives eighty years and supports lichen, bats and a dozen kinds of beetle that a dwarf orchard never sees.

03 · Pressing

Slow juice, fast neighbours.

On pressing weekends the yard fills with trailers. Apples are washed, milled, stacked in cloth and pressed under a 19th-century beam press that needs three people to lock.

Juice leaves in food-grade tanks within the hour. Members take back a share in bottles or crates, depending on whether they came to help.
