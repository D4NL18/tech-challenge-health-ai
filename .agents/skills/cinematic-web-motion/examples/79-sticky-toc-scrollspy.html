Running a cold store without losing a pallet.

Cold chain basics

Chilled goods live between 0 and 4 degrees. Every handover is logged with a probe reading, not a glance at the dashboard.

A door open for ninety seconds costs more stock than most spills. Train the night shift on that first.

Keep the dock curtain intact; replace strips at the first tear, not at the quarterly audit.

Intake checks

Check seal numbers against the waybill before the driver leaves. Photograph any mismatch while the truck is still on the bay.

Reject anything above 6 degrees at core. Write the reading on the pallet label so the dispute has evidence.

Two people sign: the receiver and the driver. One signature is a rumour.

Storage zones

Dairy sits nearest the evaporators, produce in the middle aisle, and frozen behind the second door. Never mix allergens on one rack level.

Rotate by expiry date, first expired first out, and mark the shelf edge with a coloured tape plus a printed week number.

Walk the floor with a torch once per shift. Sensors miss the corner by the pillar.

Dispatch window

Loads leave between 05:00 and 07:30 to beat city restrictions. Pick lists freeze at 04:00; late orders go on the next run.

Pre-cool the trailer for thirty minutes and log the start temperature before loading begins.

Drivers confirm door seals by number, and the dispatcher keeps the carbon copy.

Recall procedure

Quarantine by batch code the minute a supplier calls. Tag, move to the red cage, and log who touched it.

Notify affected customers within the hour, in writing, with batch numbers and delivery dates.

Close the recall only when the supplier reply and the destruction certificate are both filed.
