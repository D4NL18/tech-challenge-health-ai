Marrowby Brewing Co., Yorkshire

One hundred and thirty-seven years of the same well.

Seven moments that decided what ends up in your glass, in the order they happened.

  1. 1887

    Josiah Marrowby digs a well.

    A tanner by trade, he strikes soft water at 40 feet behind the Plough and starts brewing for the men who work the mill. The first batch is 8 barrels of mild.

    8 barrels / week
  2. 1921

    Daughters take over.

    Josiah's three granddaughters inherit after the war and rename the pale ale "Lass". It outsells the mild by 1924, and the pub signs are repainted.

    Lass Pale Ale, first brewed
  3. 1944

    The brewhouse becomes a bomb shelter.

    For nineteen months the copper room doubles as a shelter for forty families. Barrels are stacked as benches. Production halves but never stops.

    40 families sheltered
  4. 1978

    First export, to Rotterdam.

    A Dutch importer tastes the stout at a trade fair and orders 600 cases on the spot. It is the first beer to leave Yorkshire in a shipping container.

    600 cases
  5. 1996

    We nearly close.

    Supermarket pricing and a failed expansion leave the brewery eleven weeks from receivership. Forty-three regulars buy shares at £500 each to keep the doors open.

    43 regulars, £21,500
  6. 2011

    Solar roof and a new mash tun.

    The old copper is replaced with stainless, and 280 panels go on the barn. The brewery now generates about 60% of the power it burns.

    280 panels
  7. 2024

    Fifth generation at the bench.

    Josiah's great-great-granddaughter Nell brews her first solo batch, a 4.1% session bitter, from the same well. The recipe is on the label.

    Same well, same water
