Ledgerwood Housing Co-op · Annual letter

What a roof actually costs.

I · Why we exist

Ledgerwood buys tired terraced houses, repairs them to a standard we would live in ourselves, and rents them to members at a third of the local market rate. We do not sell, we do not flip, and nobody is asked to leave because the area got fashionable.

II · The numbers

We now own 63 homes across four streets. The average repair bill was 41,800 pounds per house. Rent covers every penny of upkeep, and a quarter of last year's surplus went into insulation.

III · A promise

If a boiler fails in January it is replaced within five days. If a member loses work, rent is paused for six months. If we cannot keep a promise, we say so in the next letter.

IV · Next year

In spring we will start work on Number 12 Alder Row, a forty-year-old laundry with space for nine flats, a shared drying room, and a bike store that is not a cupboard under the stairs.

Members meet on the first Thursday of the month at the Alder Row community room. Anyone on the waiting list is welcome to sit in.
