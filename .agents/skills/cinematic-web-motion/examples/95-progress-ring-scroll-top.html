Field notes from a salt marsh restoration

Survey log · Estuary 4B

The tide gauge at the eastern sluice recorded a 0.4 m rise over the past three seasons. Volunteers re-planted cordgrass along 600 metres of eroded bank before the March spring tides.

Sediment cores

Eleven cores were pulled at 20 m intervals. Organic content climbs steadily toward the creek mouth, which matches the pattern of root density measured in June.

Two cores failed the compaction check and were discarded, leaving nine usable samples for the lab.

Bird counts

Dunlin returned in numbers by late October. Redshank used the new channel edge within a week of the regrade, feeding on exposed mud at low water.

Counts were taken from the same hide at dawn to keep light conditions comparable across visits.

Planting schedule

Plugs go in at 30 cm spacing, staggered by row. A second pass in May fills gaps where gulls pulled seedlings.

Fencing stays up until the stems are knee-high, roughly twelve weeks after planting.

What we still don't know

Whether the lower terrace will hold through a surge season is open. We will resurvey the profile line every two months and publish the elevation table alongside these notes.

Funding covers monitoring until next autumn. After that, the parish council decides whether the gauge stays.
